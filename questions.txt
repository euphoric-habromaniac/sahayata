1. questions 8 to 13, for parents





    Question: Does your child have trouble sounding out new or unfamiliar words while reading?

    Rationale: Screens for difficulties in decoding—core to dyslexia and reading challenges.

    Source Type: DSM-5 criteria for reading disorders; phonological processing research.

    Question: Does your child read much more slowly than other children their age?

    Rationale: Measures reading fluency; may indicate dyslexia or general reading delay.

    Source Type: Clinical reading fluency assessments.

    Question: Does your child frequently make spelling errors or spell the same word differently within one piece of writing?

    Rationale: Flags inconsistent spelling, which is common in dyslexia and written expression disorders.

    Source Type: Spelling disorder research; DSM-5 criteria.

    Question: Does your child have difficulty understanding or remembering what they just read?

    Rationale: Assesses reading comprehension and working memory, relevant to dyslexia and broader learning concerns.

    Source Type: Comprehension tasks; working memory studies.

    Question: Does your child's writing have frequent grammar mistakes, run-on sentences, or seem disorganized?

    Rationale: Screens for written expression difficulties (dysgraphia, SLD).

    Source Type: DSM-5 written expression disorder criteria.

    Question: Is your child’s handwriting noticeably messier than that of peers?

    Rationale: Assesses graphomotor issues, suggestive of dysgraphia or fine-motor difficulties.

    Source Type: Research on handwriting and learning disorders.

    Question: Does your child frequently lose their place, skip lines, or skip words while reading?

    Rationale: Can signal attention issues, tracking problems, or dyslexia.

    Source Type: ADHD and dyslexia screener literature.

    Question: Does your child often forget instructions or need directions repeated?

    Rationale: Measures working memory and attention, pertinent to ADHD and learning disorders.

    Source Type: ADHD rating scales; cognitive assessment protocols.

    Question: Is it difficult for your child to begin or finish assignments without reminders?

    Rationale: Tests executive function and motivation, possible in ADHD, anxiety, or SLD.

    Source Type: Clinical ADHD and SLD checklists.

    Question: Does your child frequently fidget, have trouble sitting still, or leave their seat when expected to remain seated?

    Rationale: Assesses hyperactivity and impulsivity, classic in ADHD.

    Source Type: DSM-5 ADHD criteria.

    Question: Does your child seem distracted, daydream often, or struggle to pay attention to tasks?

    Rationale: Screens for inattentive behaviors, important for ADHD and other concerns.

    Source Type: ADHD evaluations; parent-report tools.

    Question: Does your child avoid reading, writing, or math homework whenever possible?

    Rationale: Behavioral avoidance signals potential anxiety or specific learning difficulties.

    Source Type: School-based and clinical screeners; DSM-5.

    Question: Does your child appear anxious, worried, or very self-critical about schoolwork or homework?

    Rationale: Looks for anxiety or low self-esteem often paired with learning disorders.

    Source Type: Social-emotional screening instruments.

    Question: Has your child voiced frustration, sadness, or embarrassment related to academics?

    Rationale: Captures mood or emotional impact of academic struggles.

    Source Type: Child mood and self-esteem research.

    Question: Is your child disorganized or prone to losing homework, books, or other school materials?

    Rationale: Screens for executive functioning difficulties, common in ADHD and learning disorders.

    Source Type: Executive functioning checklists.

    Question: Does your child blurt out answers or interrupt conversations often?

    Rationale: Tests impulsivity and self-regulation, often found in ADHD.

    Source Type: DSM-5 ADHD criteria.

    Question: Does your child find it hard to stick with a task or finish things that take a while?

    Rationale: Sustained attention and executive function screening; important for multiple disorders.

    Source Type: ADHD and educational psychology literature.

    Question: Does your child struggle to remember basic math facts or do simple calculations quickly?

    Rationale: Checks for math disability (dyscalculia).

    Source Type: DSM-5 math disorder criteria; achievement testing.

    Question: Does your child become frustrated or give up quickly when academic tasks are challenging?

    Rationale: Screens for emotional/behavioral responses to learning difficulties.

    Source Type: Observational checklists for motivation and mood.

    Question: Does your child avoid socializing, rarely has friends, or struggles with peer relationships?

    Rationale: Looks for social withdrawal or social anxiety, which may co-occur with learning challenges.

    Source Type: Social-emotional screeners; parent observation tools.

    Question: Does your child have trouble following and remembering oral directions?

    Rationale: Tests auditory working memory and processing.

    Source Type: Working memory research in SLD and ADHD.

    Question: Does your child struggle with taking turns or sharing in games or conversations?

    Rationale: Measures social skills and self-regulation.

    Source Type: Social behavior rating scales; ADHD criteria.

    Question: Are your child’s grades or skills much higher in some subjects than others?

    Rationale: Screens for specific, rather than general, learning disabilities.

    Source Type: Educational psychology literature.

    Question: Does your child have difficulty retelling a story or summarizing what they’ve learned?

    Rationale: Assesses verbal memory and language comprehension.

    Source Type: Language comprehension and memory assessments.

    Question: Is your child easily distracted by noise, activity, or other people while working?

    Rationale: Screens for attention, sensory, or processing challenges.

    Source Type: ADHD and sensory screeners.

    Question: Does your child seem restless or anxious during homework or extended reading/writing tasks?

    Rationale: Identifies anxiety, hyperactivity, or academic stress indicators.

    Source Type: DSM-5 anxiety/ADHD criteria.

    Question: Does your child frequently complain of headaches, stomachaches, or physical discomfort during schoolwork?

    Rationale: Screens for somatic symptoms related to anxiety or stress.

    Source Type: Mental health and somatic disorder research.

    Question: Does your child frequently ask for reassurance about their ability or performance in school?

    Rationale: Looks for low self-esteem, anxiety, or perfectionism tied to learning or emotional struggles.

    Source Type: Child behavior and emotional screeners.

    Question: Does your child have trouble recalling basic information, like days of the week or multiplication facts?

    Rationale: Checks basic information retention, key in various learning disorders.

    Source Type: Learning disorder diagnostic criteria.

    Question: Does your child’s mood seem to change often, especially during or after schoolwork?

    Rationale: Assesses emotional reactivity, which can indicate underlying stress or learning frustration.

    Source Type: Mood disorder screening tools; parent questionnaires.

for teachers



Question: Does the student often struggle to sound out or decode unfamiliar words when reading?

Rationale: Assesses reading-specific decoding difficulties, classic in dyslexia.

Source Type: DSM-5 criteria for reading; phonological processing tasks.

Question: Does the student have noticeably slower reading fluency compared to classmates?

Rationale: Measures word reading speed; a core dyslexia marker but also relevant for general learning challenges.

Source Type: Reading fluency subtests; clinical observation.

Question: Does the student frequently make the same spelling mistakes or spell the same word differently within a single assignment?

Rationale: Screens for inconsistent, phonologically weak spelling (dyslexia, written expression disorder).

Source Type: DSM-5 spelling criteria; writing sample analysis.

Question: Does the student have difficulty understanding or remembering what was just read?

Rationale: Assesses reading comprehension and working memory, relevant to both dyslexia and other cognitive difficulties.

Source Type: Reading comprehension tasks; cognitive assessment literature.

Question: Does the student’s written work contain frequent grammar errors, incomplete thoughts, or disorganized structure?

Rationale: Measures written expression difficulties, possible in language-based learning disorders or inattentive presentations of ADHD.

Source Type: DSM-5 criteria for written expression disorder; peer-reviewed studies.

Question: Does the student have noticeably messy or hard-to-read handwriting compared to peers?

Rationale: Assesses graphomotor deficits, often present in dysgraphia and sometimes seen with ADHD.

Source Type: Clinical observation; dysgraphia research.

Question: Does the student often lose track of their place when reading or skip lines/words?

Rationale: Can reflect attention, visual processing, or dyslexia-related tracking issues.

Source Type: Reading error analysis; ADHD and dyslexia screening studies.

Question: Does the student frequently forget instructions or need them repeated?

Rationale: Tests working memory and attention, important for both SLD and ADHD.

Source Type: ADHD rating scales; working memory assessments.

Question: Does the student have difficulty starting or completing assignments without repeated prompting?

Rationale: Suggests executive functioning or motivation issues, frequently seen with ADHD or mood problems.

Source Type: ADHD and executive function inventories.

Question: Does the student fidget, leave their seat at inappropriate times, or have trouble staying seated during lessons?

Rationale: Reflects hyperactivity/impulsivity, hallmark of ADHD.

Source Type: DSM-5 ADHD criteria; teacher behavior rating scales.

Question: Does the student daydream, appear distracted, or struggle to focus on lessons or tasks?

Rationale: Screens for inattentiveness, possible in ADHD, anxiety, or learning disorders.

Source Type: ADHD evaluations; classroom observation protocols.

Question: Does the student avoid reading, writing, or math tasks whenever possible?

Rationale: Behavioral avoidance often signals anxiety or specific learning challenges.

Source Type: School-based screening tools; DSM-5 for SLDs and anxiety.

Question: Does the student appear anxious, worried, or overly self-critical about schoolwork?

Rationale: Screens for symptoms of anxiety or low self-esteem, often co-occurring with learning disabilities.

Source Type: Social-emotional screening instruments; anxiety disorder research.

Question: Has the student expressed frustration, sadness, or embarrassment related to academic tasks?

Rationale: Measures emotional impact and internalizing symptoms associated with academic struggles.

Source Type: Mood disorder and self-esteem scales for children.

Question: Does the student have difficulty organizing materials (e.g., losing homework, messy desk)?

Rationale: Screens for executive functioning or inattentive behaviors, common in ADHD and some learning disorders.

Source Type: ADHD/executive function checklists; educational psychology guidelines.

Question: Does the student speak or blurt out answers without raising their hand or listening for instructions?

Rationale: Impulsivity and poor self-regulation, seen in ADHD and some behavioral disorders.

Source Type: DSM-5 ADHD criteria.

Question: Does the student have trouble staying on task or completing multi-step activities?

Rationale: Tests sustained attention and executive function, relevant to multiple learning and behavioral disorders.

Source Type: ADHD/school observation tools.

Question: Does the student struggle to remember basic math facts or have slow math computation skills?

Rationale: Screens for math-based learning disorder (dyscalculia).

Source Type: DSM-5 math disorder criteria; standardized math achievement tests.

Question: Does the student display frequent avoidance, frustration, or “giving up” with challenging schoolwork?

Rationale: Behavioral response to repeated academic struggle; flags possible SLDs, anxiety, or low mood.

Source Type: Observational checklists; motivational research in education.

Question: Does the student seem withdrawn, rarely participate, or have difficulty making friends?

Rationale: Reflects possible social anxiety, depression, or withdrawal due to academic struggles.

Source Type: Social-emotional and peer relationship screeners.

Question: Does the student have difficulty following and remembering oral directions?

Rationale: Tests auditory working memory and attention, critical for learning and often impaired in SLD and ADHD.

Source Type: Working memory tasks; classroom observation.

Question: During group tasks, does the student have trouble cooperating or waiting their turn?

Rationale: Assesses social skills, impulsivity, and executive function.

Source Type: Behavior and social skills rating scales.

Question: Does the student show inconsistent academic performance across different subjects or tasks?

Rationale: Screens for specific learning profiles, which help distinguish SLD from generalized cognitive or motivational issues.

Source Type: Educational psychology research; teacher rating scales.

Question: Does the student have difficulty retelling a story or summarizing information they have just learned?

Rationale: Evaluates language comprehension and verbal memory.

Source Type: Reading and listening comprehension assessments.

Question: Does the student become easily overwhelmed or distracted by noise or activity around them?

Rationale: Auditory or sensory distractibility, associated with ADHD, anxiety, or processing issues.

Source Type: Sensory processing and attention screeners.

Question: Does the student appear restless or agitated during quiet, independent work periods?

Rationale: Screens for hyperactivity or anxiety.

Source Type: DSM-5 ADHD/anxiety criteria; teacher observation.

Question: Does the student express physical complaints (e.g., headache, stomachache) during academic activities?

Rationale: Somatic symptoms may signal anxiety, stress, or emotional impact of learning challenges.

Source Type: Child mental health and psychosomatic assessment literature.

Question: Does the student require frequent reassurance about their performance or abilities?

Rationale: Indicates low self-esteem or anxiety, often secondary to repeated learning challenges.

Source Type: Child self-esteem and anxiety screening protocols.

Question: Does the student have trouble recalling basic facts (like days of the week or multiplication tables)?

Rationale: Basic fact retrieval deficits, possible in SLD (especially dyscalculia or dyslexia).

Source Type: Standard academic screening; learning disorder criteria.

Question: Does the student show frequent mood changes, irritability, or signs of distress during school activities?

Rationale: Emotional regulation issues may be linked to underlying anxiety, depression, or frustration with school demands.

Source Type: Mood disorder screening tools; teacher mental health observations.
